# Specificity beats spectacle in 2026 portfolios

In late 2026 the developer portfolios that work best are **fast, text-forward sites that lead with a specific role statement, show two to four deeply documented projects, and add one cheap signature detail**. The immersive 3D homepage still wins awards, but it is a niche for creative developers. The market has also changed underneath portfolios. Entry-level software employment for 22–25-year-olds has fallen roughly **16–20% relative to the counterfactual**, and **85% of developers now use AI coding tools**. Clean-looking apps are no longer scarce, so reviewers look for evidence that AI cannot fake cheaply: shipped and maintained work, documented decisions and tradeoffs, real metrics, and a recognizably human voice. The biggest new risk is looking generic. "AI-generated" is now the insult, and heavily AI-written application materials rank as the **#2 hiring red flag (49% of hiring managers)**. The visual clichés of 2023–25 (purple gradient heroes, floating particles and globes, typewriter taglines, equal-weight bento grids, Tailwind/shadcn defaults) now read as template or AI output. For a from-scratch redesign of this React + Vite site, the evidence points to a **content-first Astro 7 rebuild**. It should have a restrained custom visual system, a uniform case-study template, a dated "lab" for interactive proof, and motion handled mostly by native CSS view transitions and scroll-driven animations rather than WebGL.

## Reviewers skim for seconds, then engineers read for minutes

No one has measured how recruiters actually look at developer portfolios. The closest proxy is TheLadders' resume eye-tracking: **about 6 seconds on the first screen (2012), and 7.4 seconds in the 2018 follow-up**. About 80% of that time went to titles, companies, dates and education, even though recruiters said they spent 4–5 minutes ([TheLadders via BU](https://www.bu.edu/com/files/2018/10/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf)). Portfolio-specific guidance agrees on the order of magnitude. A 2026 guide says a reviewer should find what they need in **about 30 seconds**. The same guide says recruiters click through to personal sites often for frontend roles and rarely for backend or infra roles ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)). Another practitioner puts it bluntly: "If the first link is dead, they don't click the second" ([DEV](https://dev.to/thong_nguyen_83f79b7c77db/developer-portfolio-examples-that-prove-you-can-ship-not-just-code-1pnk)).

Hiring managers are split on how much a site matters. On Hacker News some "always look at personal websites," others "wouldn't spend any time on a portfolio," and one admitted being "more critical" of a candidate because of their site ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41656015)). An older 2021 survey of 60+ React hiring leads found most would look at an inexperienced candidate's site. Many also said not having one would not hurt, and that a poorly designed site with broken links "can even backfire" ([Profy.dev](https://dev.to/profydev/this-survey-among-60-hiring-managers-reveals-don-t-waste-your-time-on-a-react-portfolio-website-17ge)). The practical reading is that **a portfolio can lose more points than it gains**. Visits come in two stages: a recruiter skim, then an engineer who opens repos and reads code ([MentorCruise](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/how-to-build-a-software-engineer-portfolio-that-survives-code-review/)). The information architecture needs a top layer you can scan in seconds and depth one click down.

What goes in those layers is now fairly settled. The hero should name a **specific role in first person** ("backend engineer" beats "software developer"). Third-person "[Name] is a passionate developer…" bios read like someone else wrote them ([slategit](https://slategit.com/blog/portfolio-hero-section-headline-that-gets-interviews)). "Passionate and dedicated" openers now read as ChatGPT output ([Nkosi](https://portfolio-v2-gamma-one.vercel.app/blog/why-most-developer-portfolios-feel-boring)). For projects, the consensus is **2–4 finished, original projects** (six at most). Each should be written up as a case study: problem, your role, key decisions and tradeoffs, outcome, and a live demo or video plus a repo with a real README. The rule is "every claim needs an exhibit a stranger can open" ([Underdog.io](https://underdog.io/blog/software-engineer-portfolio); [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)). Underdog also suggests a "production note" per project covering reliability, security, observability, cost, latency, ownership and scale. Projects that can't speak honestly to at least three of these shouldn't be featured. The cut list is just as consistent: **self-rated skill bars** (an "80%" has no reference point), unlinked logo walls of technologies, tutorial clones, stale content, contact forms in place of a visible email, and heavy animation that slows load ([DEV audit of 50 portfolios](https://dev.to/robobobboy/i-audited-50-developer-portfolios-heres-what-they-all-get-wrong-j84); [DEV](https://dev.to/qawalah/3-portfolio-mistakes-hiring-managers-spot-instantly-32ma); [Scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/web-developer-portfolio-mistakes/)). Writing helps when it is real and costs you when it is a stub. HN anecdotes credit blogs with offers and interviewer conversations, and a "TODO" stub site counts against you ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41656015)). "3 strong posts beat 30 weak ones" ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474627/personal-website-portfolio-engineers/)).

## The admired sites carry almost no portfolio chrome

Sixteen widely admired sites were fetched live in November 2026. They fall into three archetypes. **Senior minimal** sites (leerob.com, paco.me, emilkowal.ski, shud.in, antfu.me) are a short first-person bio that names the employer, plus link lists. Lee Robinson's homepage has no projects section at all, just notes and dated essays ([leerob.com](https://leerob.com)). Paco Coursey lists three projects as one-liners, and each one (cmdk, next-themes) is something other developers use every day ([paco.me](https://paco.me)). **Craft-driven design engineers** let a gallery of prototypes do the arguing. Rauno Freiberg's /craft page holds about **70 dated entries from 2021 to 2026**, each labelled "View Prototype," "View Production" or "Read Essay" ([rauno.me/craft](https://rauno.me/craft)). Jhey Tompkins publishes prototypes at year-pathed URLs and adds live weather, Steam and Spotify widgets ([jhey.dev](https://jhey.dev)). **Creative immersive** sites make the site itself the demo. Bruno Simon's driving-game portfolio was rebuilt for WebGPU via Three.js TSL and released as MIT open source ([bruno-simon.com](https://bruno-simon.com); [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/10/06/self-doubt-and-the-quest-for-fun-how-i-ended-up-turning-my-portfolio-into-a-game/)).

Three patterns run across all of them. First, credibility comes from **external artifacts** (open source, employers, essays), not from how the site looks. Full case studies are actually rare among famous engineers, because their work is already public. Second, nearly every site has **one memorable hook** that isn't the project list: Brittany Chiang's TARDIS link to past versions of her site ([brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com)), Josh Comeau's sound effects with a "Disable sounds" toggle ([joshwcomeau.com](https://www.joshwcomeau.com)), Brian Lovin's "briOS" app shell with an llms.txt ([brianlovin.com](https://brianlovin.com)), and Maggie Appleton's notes organized by how certain she is of them ([maggieappleton.com](https://maggieappleton.com)). Third, the site's job follows the owner's current goal. Emil Kowalski, Josh Comeau and Kent C. Dodds run course funnels, and Delba de Oliveira turned her site into a job-seeking page whose headline CTA is the role she wants ([delba.dev](https://delba.dev)). A caveat matters here. These people have famous OSS and big audiences, so the senior-minimal approach works for them partly *because* their proof lives elsewhere. A less-known developer who copies the minimalism but has no comparable proof ends up with an empty page. Their proof has to live on the site, which is why case studies matter more for them.

Recurring secondary pages include Now, Uses/Stack, Guestbook, Craft/Lab, TIL, AMA, Talks, Colophon and archived past versions. The /now convention (2,300+ sites on [nownownow.com](https://nownownow.com/about)) is a personality signal, not a hiring signal, and a stale one hurts more than it helps.

## Restraint, one accent and native motion define the 2026 look

The best hard data on typography comes from Muzli's October 2026 study of **2,190 gallery-featured sites**, measured with the fonts Chrome actually rendered ([Muzli](https://muz.li/blog/font-pairings-2026/)). **Inter leads at 13%**. Geist (3.5%) and Geist Mono (4.1%) are concentrated on SaaS sites. Paid grotesks such as PP Neue Montreal, Neue Haas, Suisse and ABC Diatype signal design polish, and **67% of headline fonts are paid or custom**. Sixty percent of sites use one family. Among sites that pair two, a serif headline over sans body makes up 35% of pairs, led by Instrument Serif, and Muzli warns it could become a cliché next year. **23% add a mono face**, mostly for labels, navigation and dates. The typical hierarchy is about **64px headlines over 16px body**, usually at regular weight (42% at 400) with tighter-than-default tracking (58%). The practical takeaway: Inter or Geist alone reads as "competent default," a refined grotesk with a mono accent reads as "design engineer," and Instrument Serif italics in a hero read as 2025.

For layout, the mainstream is minimal and content-first ([Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/developer-portfolios/); [Scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/minimal-web-developer-portfolio-examples/)). The Brittany Chiang v4 sticky split layout has been forked so often that it reads as a template ([bchiang7/v4](https://github.com/bchiang7/v4)). Bento grids are the contested case. Muzli still calls them efficient and popular ([Muzli bento](https://muz.li/blog/bento-ui-grids/)), but a catalog of "AI smells" names **"Bento Box Sprawl"**: equal-weight cards that "read as inventory rather than argument" ([aismells](https://aismells.pages.dev/bento-box-sprawl.html)). Bento works when the cards have real hierarchy and becomes a negative signal when every section gets the same tile. On color, dark mode with a toggle is expected. Restrained palettes (monochrome or earth tones with one accent) are the quality signal, and the **purple/violet gradient is a named cliché** ([Nkosi](https://portfolio-v2-gamma-one.vercel.app/blog/why-most-developer-portfolios-feel-boring)). There is no sourced data on the dark/light split. That a light, paper-toned editorial palette now stands out more than another dark site is an inference.

Motion has become cheap to do well. **Same-document View Transitions reached Baseline in October 2025** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available?authuser=0)). Scroll-driven animations ship in Chrome, Firefox and Safari ([web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/scroll-driven-animations)). Cross-document transitions and anchor positioning are Interop 2026 focus areas, so treat them as progressive enhancement ([web.dev Interop 2026](https://web.dev/blog/interop-2026)). The 2026 benchmark for tasteful motion is something like Joffrey Spitzer's "Minimalist Astro + GSAP build with reveals, flip transitions and subtle motion" ([Codrops Feb 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/)). Typewriter heroes draw open complaints ("please no more typewriter animation") ([DEV](https://dev.to/ioanat94/dos-and-donts-of-creating-your-portfolio-website-2h4p/comments)). 3D helps when it *is* the concept and shows real craft. Decorative particles and floating globes are clichés, and long load times were the top feedback on a Three.js portfolio posted to the three.js forum ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/my-3d-developer-portfolio-built-with-three-js-vite/82753); [Nkosi](https://portfolio-v2-gamma-one.vercel.app/blog/why-most-developer-portfolios-feel-boring)).

## Novelty is forgiven when it adds and punished when it gates

The clearest community evidence is the Hacker News thread on Bruno Simon's 2025 rebuild (**777 points, 188 comments**). Commenters praised the craft. They also complained about long loads with no progress bar, phone stutter and "CPU usage is almost 100% all the time." One called it "very impressive as an art project, but terrible as an actual home page," and others noted that it markets his Three.js courses ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46206531)). A 2026 terminal-style portfolio got praise for its easter eggs and mobile support. Its author admitted that an earlier version with real shell commands was "un-navigatable for non-technical folks," and commenters asked for an HTML CV instead of a download ([HN via hn.nuxt.dev](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/47205127)). The pattern is consistent: **novelty is welcome when it is additive and cheap, and resented when it stands between the visitor and the content**. Design-community details are the low-risk version. Examples are sound on hover and click as part of the interaction design ([Codrops R—K '26](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/07/r-k-26-the-thinking-and-code-behind-a-portfolio-led-by-presence/)), Cmd+K menus, and live multiplayer cursors, which are now a one-line add via PartyKit ([PartyKit](https://blog.partykit.io/posts/cursor-party)). Every one of these needs an off switch. One blogger added a footer toggle because many readers disliked custom cursors ([dev.to](https://dev.to/brennan/building-my-blog-from-scratch-indieweb-new-features-and-three-months-of-iterations-4b1d)). Spotify "now playing" widgets and GitHub contribution graphs now ship pre-bundled in templates ([dev.to](https://dev.to/jqueryscript/cooked-folio-a-portfolio-template-with-spotifygithubdiscord-integrated-1gd0)). That probably makes them read as template features rather than personality, though no source calls them cliché outright.

The audiences really do diverge. Codrops and Awwwards still feature sound- and motion-rich portfolios all year, with developer-portfolio Site of the Day wins through November 2026 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration_search/developer/)). Job-seeker guides, meanwhile, praise sites with "No animations, no parallax, no glassmorphism" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@coders.stop/building-a-developer-portfolio-that-gets-interviews-6b523901e89a)). One 2026 review warns that a "studio aesthetic" can confuse recruiters who are scanning for "engineer" ([slategit](https://slategit.com/blog/developer-portfolio-examples-that-got-hired)). How much spectacle is right depends on the target role. The compromise that holds up is a fast default path with optional depth layers.

## AI changed what counts as proof more than how sites look

The AI-era backlash is aimed at defaults, not at AI itself. "The 'AI look' isn't really about AI. It's about defaults": Tailwind defaults, shadcn components, a blue or purple primary and a standard hero ([dev.to](https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh)). 21st.dev names **generic copy as the strongest tell**. Its rule is to "borrow composition from components but write the content yourself" ([21st.dev](https://21st.dev/blog/website-not-look-ai-generated)). Mainstream design press is running the same story ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/ai/everything-looks-the-same-now-what); [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/ai-design-isnt-ugly-it-s-fluent-and-thats-the-problem-131b2f4eb78c)). Hiring managers report that "all the candidates are starting to look the same" ([CNBC via NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/business/money-report/the-biggest-red-flag-this-hiring-expert-sees-in-job-seekers-all-the-candidates-are-starting-to-look-the-same/3705906/?os=app)). AI-heavy materials are now the #2 red flag ([CPA Practice Advisor / Resume Genius](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/08/13/ai-written-applications-are-now-the-2-red-flag-in-hiring/188492/)). For AI-assisted projects, Underdog recommends an "AI evidence block": how requirements were split, which generated code was rejected, how hallucinations were checked, what stayed human-owned, and latency and inference cost ([Underdog.io](https://underdog.io/blog/software-engineer-portfolio)). A thin LLM-API wrapper is the new tutorial clone.

The agent-facing features sort out clearly. **"Chat with my resume" bots are commoditized.** PortfolioOS, Prezumi, GPTFolio and many DIY RAG tutorials all offer them ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/portfolioos-2); [dev.to](https://dev.to/okasputra/stop-sending-static-resumes-how-i-built-a-chat-with-my-resume-bot-nextjs-rag-4kdm)). The only evidence recruiters like them is a single vendor-review anecdote, and skeptics say reviewers have seen "the same chatbot clone five hundred times" ([Learnist](https://www.learnist.org/signs-ai-portfolio-project-wont-impress-hiring-managers/)). A bot also adds ongoing API cost and a prompt-injection attack surface ([CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4206304/your-ai-hiring-tool-isnt-an-hr-problem-its-a-security-one.html)). Hidden prompts aimed at AI screeners don't work and probably lead to rejection ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/hidden-ai-prompts-in-resume)). **llms.txt costs about 30 minutes and has unproven value.** Perplexity says it reads the file, Anthropic and OpenAI haven't confirmed runtime use, and Google says you don't need new machine-readable files ([suganthan.com](https://suganthan.com/blog/how-to-make-website-agent-ready/)). A personal MCP server only makes sense if there is a real tool surface behind it. David Hoang deliberately skipped one because his site isn't "an app with an API surface worth operating and securing" ([davidhoang.com](https://www.davidhoang.com/writing/optimizing-for-agents)), and a stub MCP server that just redirects to static files is worse than having none.

## The stack question has a clear 2026 answer for content sites

**Astro 7** shipped June 22, 2026 and is at 7.4 as of October. It brings Vite 8/Rolldown, a Rust compiler and Markdown pipeline, and reports 15–61% faster builds ([Astro 7](https://astro.build/blog/astro-7/); [Astro blog](https://astro.build/blog/)). Astro 6 stabilized a built-in Fonts API, a CSP API and Live Content Collections ([Astro 6](https://astro.build/blog/astro-6)). Astro **leads meta-framework satisfaction** in State of JS 2025. Next.js leads usage at 59% but trails Astro by 39 points on satisfaction ([State of JS 2025](https://2025.stateofjs.com/en-US/libraries/meta-frameworks/)). Next.js 16.4 is mature, but it now runs a monthly security-release program after a critical RSC CVE in December 2025 ([Next.js blog](https://nextjs.org/blog)). SvelteKit 3 and Nuxt 5 were still pre-release in late 2026 ([Svelte](https://svelte.dev/blog/whats-new-in-svelte-august-2026); [Nuxt](https://nuxt.com/docs/community/roadmap)). Contentlayer is abandoned ([wisp.blog](https://www.wisp.blog/blog/contentlayer-has-been-abandoned-what-are-the-alternatives)). On the supporting side, Tailwind is at v4.3 ([Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/tailwindcss-v4-3)). **GSAP is fully free, including SplitText and MorphSVG** ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free)). Lenis respects reduced-motion by default ([Lenis](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/main/README.md)). WCAG 3 is still a Working Draft ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG3)).

## Implications for a from-scratch redesign

The current site is a React 19 + Vite 6 single-page app with React Three Fiber and postprocessing, GSAP, a `devicons-react` icon set (likely a logo wall in `Skills.jsx`), and a just-deleted `StarsCanvas.jsx`. That last deletion already points the way the research does. The recommendations below are opinionated. Where they go beyond the sources, that is judgment.

### Positioning: one sentence a recruiter can repeat

Pick a single target role and say it in the first line, in first person. The formula is **role + specialty + proof**: "I'm a frontend engineer who builds data-heavy interfaces. Most recently I shipped X at Y." Follow it with one line of logistics (location or time zone, availability, "open to full-time roles") and two CTAs: a visible, copyable email and an HTML resume with a PDF download. Write all copy yourself, in your own voice, with concrete nouns and numbers. Run every sentence through one test: could a hundred other portfolios say the same thing? If yes, rewrite it or delete it. Since the market rewards proof over breadth, position around the **two things you can defend in an interview**, not a stack inventory.

### Sitemap

| Page | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `/` Home | The 30-second skim | Hero (role, proof, availability, CTAs), 3 featured work rows, short "currently" line, latest 2–3 writing/lab items. No tech logo wall. |
| `/work` + `/work/[slug]` | Depth for the engineer reviewer | 2–4 case studies, one uniform template (below). An index row list of smaller projects. |
| `/lab` | Dated interactive proof | Small prototypes and experiments with dates and a Prototype / Production / Note label, in the style of rauno.me/craft. Cheap to grow, and it shows you keep practicing. |
| `/writing` (optional at launch) | Public thinking | Ship it only once you have 2–3 real posts. Never launch an empty blog. |
| `/about` | The human | Short bio, experience timeline that matches the resume, skills grouped and linked to the projects that prove them. |
| `/resume` | HTML resume + PDF | Commenters ask for HTML instead of a forced download. |
| `/now` (optional) | Presence | Only if you'll update it. Show the "last updated" date. |
| `/colophon` | Craft transparency | Stack, fonts, how it was built, what AI was used for, and a link to the source repo. |

Put contact in the footer of every page (email as text, GitHub, LinkedIn) rather than giving it its own page or a form.

### Visual direction and clichés to avoid

Aim for **editorial restraint with one owned accent**. A light, paper-toned default with a well-made dark theme (theme follows the system, with a toggle) will stand out more than another navy or black site. Use one distinctive grotesk for display and body. If budget rules out paid faces, choose something less common than Inter or Geist and customize tracking and weights. Add a mono for dates, labels and metadata, in the 23%-of-gallery-sites pattern. Use a large type scale (about 4x) at regular weight with tight tracking, not heavy mega-bold. Work rows should look like an index or list (year · role · stack · outcome), not equal cards. Get hierarchy from asymmetry and size, not from tiles. Customize the design system's tokens (color, radius, type, spacing) before building any component, so nothing ships with library defaults.

The avoid list: purple/violet gradient heroes and gradient text on black. Stars, particles, floating globes and decorative R3F scenes (remove R3F and postprocessing entirely unless 3D becomes the concept). Typewriter or rotating-role taglines. "Hi, I'm X, a passionate developer." Skill bars and the devicons logo wall. The v4 sticky split clone. Equal-weight bento sprawl. Stock shadcn styling. Instrument Serif italic heroes. AOS-style fade-up on every element. A custom cursor that replaces the native one. Scroll-jacking. Spotify and contribution-graph widgets used as filler. A "Let's Connect" form.

### Project presentation format

Every case study uses the same skeleton so reviewers can scan it. Order: a one-line outcome headline. Then a meta strip: role, team size, timeline, stack. Then a short looping video or screenshot. Then the problem in 2–3 sentences, 2–3 key decisions each with the tradeoff it involved, and the result with a metric, users or adoption if any exist. Then a production note (pick the three or more of reliability, security, observability, cost, latency and scale you can speak to honestly), an AI evidence block if AI was involved (what you delegated, what you rejected, how you verified), and "what I'd do differently." End with **Live · Code · Write-up** links. Before launch, check that each link resolves and that each repo's README passes a clean-clone test. On Home, each featured project is a single row: outcome headline, meta, and links. Put the strongest, most role-relevant project first.

### Recommended stack

| Layer | Choice | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Framework | **Astro 7.x** | Zero JS by default. Content collections for case studies, lab and writing. Built-in Fonts and CSP APIs. Existing React components can be reused as islands. Leads developer satisfaction. Next.js 16 is the alternative only if you specifically want RSC on your resume or need app features (auth, DB). |
| Interactive islands | React 19 (via `@astrojs/react`) | Reuse existing skills and components where an island is needed (Cmd+K, lab demos). Hydrate on idle or visible. |
| Styling | Tailwind v4.3 with a custom token layer, or plain modern CSS | Already in the project. Define the theme in CSS-first `@theme` so nothing uses default palettes. Container queries and `:has()` are Baseline. |
| Motion | Native View Transitions + CSS scroll-driven animations first; GSAP (free, incl. SplitText) for the one or two choreographed moments | GSAP is already a dependency. Wrap everything in `prefers-reduced-motion` and `@supports`. Skip Lenis unless you can justify it. |
| Content | Astro content collections (MDX), with Zod schemas for project frontmatter (role, timeline, links, metrics) | The schema enforces the uniform case-study template. Use the opt-in remark/rehype pipeline if you need syntax-highlighting plugins. Avoid Contentlayer. |
| Hosting | Cloudflare (Workers/Pages static assets) | Unlimited static bandwidth, commercial use allowed, natural fit with Astro. Vercel Hobby also works for a non-commercial site. |
| Analytics | A privacy-friendly, cookieless option (e.g. Plausible, Umami or Cloudflare Web Analytics) | Avoids a consent banner. Pricing wasn't researched. |
| OG images | Build-time generated per page (Satori-based) | Every shared case-study link gets a branded card. Not researched in depth. |

### Signature interactions that are cheap and never gate content

Pick **one** as the signature and at most two supporting details. Each must be skippable, keyboard-accessible, and turned off under reduced motion. First, **view-transition morphs** from a work row into its case-study hero: native, nearly free, and it makes the site feel crafted. Second, a **Cmd+K command menu** for navigating pages, copying the email, toggling the theme and opening the resume. It's useful for power users and invisible to everyone else. Third, the **dated `/lab` itself**, where each entry is a small interaction you built (a toggle, a gesture, a data-viz micro-demo). This is the strongest proof of front-end taste and it keeps growing. Fourth, **click-to-copy email** with a satisfying confirmation state, in the style of rauno.me. Fifth, an **archive of past versions** ("see v1"), which turns the old R3F site into a story instead of throwing it away. Optional extras with an off switch: subtle UI sound with a mute toggle remembered across visits, a time-of-day accent color, and a guestbook or multiplayer cursors on the lab page only. **Do not build** a chat-with-resume bot or an MCP server as recruiting features. If you want an AI project, build one with real evals, cost and failure handling, and present it as a case study.

### Performance, accessibility and SEO checklist

| Area | Target / action |
|---|---|
| Core Web Vitals | LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at p75. Aim for Lighthouse 95–100 in the lab. Set a JS budget: Home ships close to zero JS outside the Cmd+K island. |
| Media | Use Astro `<Image>` with AVIF/WebP, explicit width and height, and lazy loading below the fold. Case-study videos as muted, short, `preload="none"` loops with posters. |
| Fonts | Self-host via the Astro Fonts API. Subset, use `font-display: swap`, and preload only the display face. |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 AA: skip link, visible focus not hidden by sticky headers, 24×24 px minimum targets, 4.5:1 contrast in both themes, keyboard-operable Cmd+K and menus, real alt text, semantic headings, reduced-motion handling for every animation path, and no information conveyed by motion or sound alone. Test with a screen reader once. |
| SEO | Unique titles and descriptions, canonical URLs, a sitemap, `robots.txt`, JSON-LD `Person` on Home and `CreativeWork`/`Article` on case studies, OG and Twitter cards, and a clean URL slug per project. Pages are server-rendered or static, never SPA-only. |
| Agent-readability (cheap) | `llms.txt` summarizing who you are, your role and your case-study links. Takes about 30 minutes. No hidden prompt text, ever. |
| Hygiene | Link checker in CI. Every page shows a date or "last updated." 404 page. Security headers via the CSP API. A 15-minute quarterly upkeep pass. |

### Phased build order

| Phase | Deliverable | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Content first | Write the hero sentence, pick 2–4 projects, draft case studies with the template, fix READMEs, update the resume | You can explain each project's decisions and tradeoffs aloud. Every link works. |
| 1. Skeleton | Astro 7 project, content collections with schemas, routes from the sitemap, unstyled but complete pages, deploy to Cloudflare | Real content live at a preview URL. Lighthouse 100 on unstyled pages. |
| 2. Visual system | Type, palette tokens (light + dark), spacing scale, index-row work list, case-study layout | Passes the "would 100 other sites look like this?" test. Contrast passes in both themes. |
| 3. Motion and signature | View transitions, one choreographed GSAP moment, the chosen signature interaction, reduced-motion paths | CWV still green. Everything works with motion off and keyboard only. |
| 4. SEO and polish | Metadata, JSON-LD, OG images, sitemap, llms.txt, colophon, 404, link checker in CI | All checklist items done. Old site archived as "v1." |
| 5. Grow | `/lab` entries monthly, writing when real, `/now` if maintained | Something dated in the last 90 days, always. |

## Conclusion

The main change in 2026 is that a polished surface no longer proves anything, because AI tools and templates produce it for everyone. That moves the portfolio's job from showing that you can build a nice website to showing **judgment that a stranger can verify**: documented decisions, honest tradeoffs, maintained work, dated practice and a voice that is clearly a person's. Visual craft still matters, but now as a signal of taste and care (custom tokens, deliberate type, one well-made interaction) rather than as spectacle. Spectacle has become both a performance liability and a recognizable template.

For this redesign, the biggest gains are not in the framework choice. They come from writing four strong case studies and one specific hero sentence before touching layout, and from replacing the 3D-and-logo-wall approach with a dated lab that keeps proving skill after launch. The evidence has real limits. No one has measured portfolio review behavior, recruiter opinions come mostly from blogs and HN anecdotes, and admired-site visuals were not verified in a rendered browser. Treat the specific recommendations as strong defaults, not proven conversion tactics, and adjust how much spectacle to include to the role you are targeting: more for creative or frontend roles, less for backend.
